Thirteen letters carry Paul's name. Hebrews does not. Modern study usually sorts them into three piles plus Hebrews. The piles are scholarly habits, not a fourth gospel.

## The seven almost no one contests

Romans, 1–2 Corinthians, Galatians, Philippians, 1 Thessalonians, Philemon.

Early writers quote them as Paul. The voice, the fights, the travel grit, and the theology hang together. If "Paul" means anything as a historical writer, it means this set. Teach them as his without apology.

## How the argument actually works

People are not only counting votes. They are weighing:

- **Name on the page.** Eleven letters open "Paul…" Hebrews does not.
- **Early reception.** Cited as Paul by the late second century (Irenaeus, Tertullian, Clement of Alexandria, Muratorian list for most of the thirteen). That is not nothing.
- **Vocabulary and sentence shape.** Pastorals and Ephesians use a thicker Greek and a different word-stock than Galatians or 1 Corinthians.
- **Church picture.** House-tables and gifts in the seven; overseers, widows-lists, and "deposit of teaching" in the Pastorals.
- **Hope-clock.** Imminent coming and a still-future rising of bodies in the seven; realized "already raised with him" language in Colossians/Ephesians; a calendar of signs in 2 Thessalonians.
- **Itinerary.** Pastorals assume a free Paul placing men in Ephesus and Crete after Acts ends.
- **Secretaries.** Tertius writes Romans (Rom 16:22). "I, Paul, write this greeting with my own hand" (1 Cor 16:21; Gal 6:11; Col 4:18; 2 Thess 3:17) assumes someone else usually held the pen.

A secretary can explain some style shift. A secretary does not automatically explain a different church-order or a different clock. Say that out loud.

## Three chairs that keep coming back

**Chair A — all thirteen are Paul** (traditional / most evangelical classrooms).
Differences are situation, age, prison, amanuensis, and opponents. Acts 28 is not the end of his road; 1 Timothy and Titus fit a release; 2 Timothy is last. Colossians and Ephesians are the mature cosmic Christ of the same man who wrote Philippians.

**Chair B — seven plus some of the middle three** (many moderates).
Colossians and/or 2 Thessalonians may still be his; Ephesians is a circular rewrite of Colossians; Pastorals are a later Pauline school.

**Chair C — seven only, rest are school or pseudepigraphy** (critical majority on Pastorals and usually Ephesians).
Write-in-the-name-of-the-teacher was a known ancient practice. The "Paul" of the later letters is the tradition speaking after his death, often dated 80–100.

A fourth tone exists: calling the later letters **forgeries**. Use that word only if you also say the ancient church did not treat them that way, and that 2 Thess 2:2 already fears a fake letter in Paul's name. The New Testament is not naive about counterfeits.

## Letter by letter

### 2 Thessalonians
Why doubted: 2 Thess 2's "not until the apostasy and the man of sin" seems to slow 1 Thess 4–5's sudden day. Style can sound like a careful copy of 1 Thessalonians.

Why defended: the same church, a rumor that the day has come (2:1–2), a hand-greeting as the mark (3:17). A second note to correct a misread first note is how letters work.

Teach 1–2 Thessalonians together either way. Flag the chair in views, not in the opening point.

### Colossians
Why doubted: long liturgical sentences; Christ as cosmic fullness; "you have been raised" as present (2:12; 3:1); household code.

Why defended: prison with Onesimus and Archippus (Col 4:7–17; Phlm 10–12) ties it to Philemon, which almost no one denies. The Christ-hymn (1:15–20) can be Paul quoting a hymn the way he quotes in Philippians 2.

Closest sibling is Philemon plus Ephesians. That is why the file family is Phil + Col + Philemon, with Ephesians nearby.

### Ephesians
Why doubted: "to the Ephesians" is textually shaky (some early copies lack the place-name). Very long sentences. About half the lines have verbal cousins in Colossians. Generic church, little local grit. "We too lived in the passions" (2:3) sits oddly in some readers' Paul.

Why defended: early and wide reception as Paul. Same prison courier talk as Colossians (Tychicus, Eph 6:21–22; Col 4:7–8). A circular letter from Paul would look exactly this general.

Do not let the authorship fight steal 1:3–14 or 2:11–22. Those pages preach whether the pen is his or his school's.

### 1–2 Timothy and Titus
Why doubted hardest: vocabulary unique in the corpus; "sound doctrine" as a deposit; detailed overseer/deacon lists; widows; myths and genealogies; itinerary that will not sit inside Acts.

Why defended: different job — memos to coworkers, not to a whole church. Late style after years of travel. Polycarp and Irenaeus already treat them as Paul. 2 Timothy's personal debris (cloak, parchments, names) is hard to fake without purpose, and if you are inventing a Paul you may not invent a forgotten cloak at Troas.

2 Timothy is often the one even hesitant readers call "most like him." Keep the three in one file family. Name the second-career chair (see chronology brief) beside the school chair.

## Hebrews — do not smuggle it into the thirteen

No "Paul, apostle…" greeting. Greek is a higher register than the seven. Old-argument is priestly and sermonic.

Ancient chairs already split:
- East often received it as Paul (sometimes: Paul in Hebrew, Luke or Clement in Greek).
- West slower; Tertullian said Barnabas; others said Clement or Luke.
- Origen (as Eusebius quotes him): the *thoughts* are the apostle's; the *phrasing* is someone who wrote up the teacher; "who wrote the epistle, God knows." He still commends churches that hold it as Paul's because "the ancients did not pass it down as Paul's in vain."

Modern majority: author unknown. Keep Hebrews in its own file family. Do not date it on a Pauline calendar unless you first say why.

## What this does to the study files

- The seven: write as Paul. No need to reopen authorship in every doctrinal box.
- 2 Thessalonians, Colossians, Ephesians: one views-entry each. Teach the text at full weight either way.
- Pastorals: same depth as Romans. Authorship chair named once per book, not every chapter.
- Hebrews: anonymous preacher unless a session explicitly takes Origen's "thoughts of the apostle" line.
- Do not let "critical majority" or "church tradition" become the sermon. The pages still have to be read.

## A sentence that can stay in the room

If a letter wears Paul's name, the first job is to hear what that name is being used to say. The second job is to tell the truth about who may have held the pen. Those two jobs are not the same, and neither one cancels the other.
