## The whole file in Scripture

Three pages. That is the entire biblical dossier.

1. **Genesis 14:18–20** — a king-priest meets Abraham.
2. **Psalm 110:4** — YHWH swears a forever-priest "after the order of Melchizedek."
3. **Hebrews 5–7** — that oath is how a Judah-born Son can be high priest.

Do not build a fourth page out of later speculation and then preach it as Genesis.

## Genesis 14 — what the text actually does

Abraham returns from beating the eastern kings. The king of Sodom comes out. So does **Melchizedek king of Salem**, with bread and wine. He is **priest of El Elyon** (God Most High). He blesses Abram of God Most High, maker of heaven and earth, and blesses God Most High who delivered the enemies. Abram gives him a tenth of the spoils.

Then he vanishes. No father. No death. No tribe. No sequel.

Name: *malki-tsedeq* — king of righteousness. Salem is widely read as Jerusalem (Ps 76:2, "Salem… Zion"); Hebrews also hears "peace." First person in the Bible called **kohen**.

Abraham later tells Sodom he has lifted his hand to YHWH, El Elyon (Gen 14:22). The priest's God and Abraham's God are named as the same Most High.

## Psalm 110 — why a king needs this priest

Psalm 110 is the NT's most-quoted psalm. "YHWH said to my Lord: sit at my right hand." Verse 4 adds an oath God will not rethink: **you are a priest forever, after the manner / order of Melchizedek.**

Aaronic priests were not kings. Davidic kings were not Aaronic priests. Judah had no altar assignment (Heb 7:14). Melchizedek is the only OT figure who is already both. The psalm gives a Davidic "Lord" a priesthood that does not run through Levi.

Jesus uses 110:1 on the Pharisees (Matt 22:41–46). Hebrews uses 110:1 and 110:4 as the two rails under the Son: seated Lord, forever priest.

## Hebrews — how the argument runs

Hebrews does not start with Genesis. It starts with the **oath** (5:6; 6:20), then explains Genesis in chapter 7.

- Lesser is blessed by the better. Abraham paid a tenth. Levi-in-the-loins paid too (7:4–10).
- If Levi could finish people, why another order (7:11)? Change of priesthood means change of the law-arrangement (7:12).
- Jesus is Judah, not Levi. Appointment is not a flesh commandment. It is the **power of an indestructible life** (7:16).
- Levi: many priests, because death. This one remains. Unfinished intercession. Able to save to the uttermost those who come to God through him (7:23–25).
- Once he offered himself. The oath after the law appoints the Son, perfected forever (7:26–28).

"Without father, mother, genealogy, beginning of days or end of life" (7:3) is the **silence of the Genesis page** read as a type ("made like unto the Son of God"). It is not a birth certificate claiming the Salem king was a phantom or a pre-incarnate Christ unless you choose that chair and say so.

## Second Temple chairs (background, not canon)

The Bible's silence invited a crowd.

| Chair | Who | What they do with him |
|---|---|---|
| Historical Canaanite priest-king of Salem/Jerusalem | Josephus | First priest of God there; some lines even give him a first temple |
| Heavenly end-time deliverer | 11QMelchizedek (11Q13), Qumran | Jubilee, Isaiah 61 liberty, judgment on Belial's lot |
| Logos / righteous king in allegory | Philo | Priest who feeds the soul; not the Qumran warrior |
| Shem son of Noah | Targums, later rabbis | Puts him back inside Israel's family so the priesthood stays in-house |
| Type of the Son | Hebrews | Silence in Genesis + oath in the psalm → forever priest who is Jesus |

11Q13 matters because it shows Jews before Hebrews could already hear Melchizedek as more than a cameo. Hebrews does **not** copy Qumran's Belial-war. It copies the psalm's oath and Genesis's tithe.

Rabbinic Shem-identification (and the later claim that priesthood passed from him to Abraham because the blessing named Abram first) is a different use of the same thin file. Name it. Do not preach it as Genesis.

## Chairs to name once in a group

**Who was the man in Genesis 14?**
- A real priest-king of Salem who worshiped the Most High.
- A literary figure whose job is to bless Abraham and then disappear.
- (Fringe) a theophany / pre-incarnate Christ. Hebrews says *made like* the Son, which is the wrong shape for "he was the Son."

**What does "order of Melchizedek" mean?**
- Pattern: priest-king, not Levi, not dying-out of office.
- A heavenly guild Hebrews is joining to Qumran's Melchizedek. The letter's interest is Jesus, not a guild.

**Does 7:12 trash the law?**
- No. The priesthood-arrangement changes because a Judahite priest required it. The law completed nothing as a finishing instrument (7:19). Moses remains a faithful servant in the house (3:5).

## One paragraph for a group

The Bible gives Melchizedek three verses and one oath. He is king of righteousness, king of peace, priest of God Most High. Abraham pays him and is blessed — the lesser by the better. David's Lord is sworn a priest forever in that pattern, because no son of Judah could take Aaron's chair. Hebrews says that oath landed on Jesus: not a rotation of dying men, the power of an endless life, one offering, an intercession that does not clock out. The mystery is not a secret biography. It is a thin page used to seat a Son.

## Shem compared (not equated)

Genesis never says Shem is Melchizedek. The identification is a later Jewish chair (Palestinian Targums; R. Ishmael in b. Nedarim 32b). Josephus and Philo do not make the equation. Hebrews cannot make it: Shem has a full genealogy (Gen 5; 11); Melchizedek is read as without genealogy (7:3).

**Why the chair exists.** On the Masoretic numbers Shem lives 600 years and overlaps Abraham (Jerome: Shem outlives Abraham by decades). A living flood-witness in Salem would explain a priest of El Elyon who is not a pagan leftover, and would keep the first *kohen* inside Noah's blessed line.

**What the rabbis then do with Psalm 110.** Nedarim 32b: God wanted priesthood from Shem; Melchizedek/Shem blessed Abram *before* God; priesthood passed to Abraham; "after the order of Melchizedek" reread as "because of the words of Melchizedek." That is the opposite pressure from Hebrews (Melchizedek remains greater; Levi-in-the-loins paid him).

**Teach both pages.** Shem is named, dated, father of a line. Melchizedek is king-priest of Salem, undated, without a line on the page. Chronology overlap makes a meeting *possible*. It does not make the names the same. Hebrews uses the silence. The Shem-chair fills the silence. Say which job you are doing.
